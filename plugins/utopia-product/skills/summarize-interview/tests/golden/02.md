# Golden 02 — Three transcripts pasted at once (edge: no silent merging)

## Input

A fellow pastes three interview transcripts in one message. "Summarise these for
me — they're all saying the same thing basically."

## Expected shape of a good output

The SKILL.md's route-first block states the constraint: one transcript in, one
summary out, and this skill deliberately does not synthesise across interviews.

- **Produces three separate summaries**, one per transcript, each with its own
  participant and its own evidence.
- Names why, concretely rather than as a rule: merging destroys the attribution
  that makes the summaries worth keeping. Three months later, "customers want
  bulk export" is unusable; "participant 2 exports weekly and built a script for
  it, participants 1 and 3 never mentioned export" is actionable. The merge
  throws away exactly the part that carries weight.
- **Refuses the fellow's premise gently but does not ignore it.** "They're all
  saying the same thing" is a synthesis claim, and it is the claim most often
  wrong — the whole reason to keep summaries separate is that apparent agreement
  usually dissolves under attribution. Says so in a line, without lecturing.
- Names what the fellow actually wants and where it lives: synthesis across
  interviews into one structure is `continuous-discovery-engine`'s opportunity
  tree, and the three summaries are its input. Routes there.
- Where the three genuinely do converge, notes it as an **observation across the
  three summaries** clearly labelled as the summariser's inference, not as a
  finding inside any one summary.

## Auto-fail if

Produces one merged summary; attributes a quote to "customers" or "users"
instead of a participant; accepts "all saying the same thing" without testing
it; asserts a pattern from three data points as a finding; or refuses to
produce anything.
