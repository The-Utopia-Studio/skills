# Golden 03 — Half a transcript (diagnosis: thin input)

## Input

A fellow pastes about 300 words. "Here's the call — recording cut out partway.
Summarise it." The fragment covers the participant's role and the start of a
story about their current process, and stops mid-sentence.

## Expected shape of a good output

The template's `-`-for-unavailable instruction is load-bearing here, and the
temptation is to complete the story.

- **Summarises the fragment only.** Background and role are present, so they get
  filled. Current Solution is partial — the story starts but does not finish —
  so it records what was said and marks the rest `-`.
- **Problems, What-They-Like, Key Insights and Action Items are mostly `-`,**
  because the fragment does not reach them. Empty is the correct output; a
  plausible-sounding Problems section here is indistinguishable from a real one
  in three weeks.
- **Names what was lost and where.** The recording cut mid-story about the
  current process, which is precisely the section that would have carried the
  most evidence. That is worth one line so the fellow knows the summary is not a
  weak interview but a truncated one — a different problem with a different fix.
- Names the fix and that it is cheap: the participant is reachable and the
  question is narrow. A five-minute follow-up on the one unfinished story
  recovers most of the value. That beats re-running the interview.
- **Does not extrapolate the ending.** The story's direction may be obvious from
  the setup; completing it is fabrication regardless of how likely the ending is.

## Auto-fail if

Completes the unfinished story; fills Problems or Key Insights with content the
fragment does not contain; produces a full-looking summary that hides the
truncation; treats the thin input as a thin interview rather than a truncated
recording; or invents action items.
