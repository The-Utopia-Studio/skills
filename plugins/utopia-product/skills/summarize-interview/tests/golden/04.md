# Golden 04 — Internal stakeholder, not a customer (mundane/mislabelled input)

## Input

A fellow pastes a transcript. "Summarise this customer interview." Reading it,
the participant is the fellow's own head of sales describing what customers tell
him.

## Expected shape of a good output

- **Notices the mislabel and says so in one line.** This is a stakeholder
  interview, not a customer interview. It is a legitimate and useful input; it
  is not customer evidence.
- Fills the template but relabels the evidence honestly: everything the head of
  sales reports about customers is **second-hand**. Marks it as such rather than
  letting it enter the record as something a customer said.
- Names why the distinction is not pedantic: second-hand accounts are filtered
  twice — by which customers talk to sales, and by which parts sales
  remembers as important. Both filters point the same way, toward objections
  that block deals and away from problems customers have quietly stopped
  mentioning.
- **Separates the two kinds of content in the transcript**, which is the real
  work here: what the head of sales has *directly observed* (which deals
  stalled, what the objection was, what he has tried) is first-hand and strong.
  What he *reports customers as wanting* is second-hand. The summary should not
  flatten them together.
- Records the useful next step: the accounts he named are a recruiting list.
  Routes to `discovery-interview-prep` for turning them into first-hand
  interviews.
- Does not refuse or discard the transcript.

## Auto-fail if

Records second-hand reports as customer quotes; treats the transcript as
customer evidence without qualification; discards it as invalid; conflates the
head of sales's direct observations with his reports of customer wants;
invents customer names or deal values; or lectures at length instead of noting
the distinction and getting on with it.
