# Golden 01 — One transcript in, one summary out (typical)

## Input

Mentix pastes a 40-minute transcript from a shift supervisor at the pilot site.
The supervisor describes rewriting the digest's machine order every shift,
mentions keeping a paper list of "the three that always go", says the digest
"misses the ones that just came back from service", and at one point says
"honestly it's fine, it's better than nothing".

## Expected shape of a good output

- The full template filled: date, participants, background, current solution,
  what they like, problems, key insights, action items.
- **Every line under Problems and What-They-Like traces to something said.**
  Quote or near-quote. The rewrite-the-order behaviour and the
  just-back-from-service gap are both direct.
- **The paper list is recognised as the strongest evidence in the transcript.**
  It is a workaround the supervisor built and maintains with her own time —
  behaviour, not opinion. It belongs in Current Solution and it outranks
  everything she said.
- **"Honestly it's fine, it's better than nothing" is not recorded as
  satisfaction.** It is politeness, and the transcript contradicts it — someone
  who rewrites the output every shift and maintains a parallel paper list is
  not satisfied. A good summary notes the contradiction rather than averaging
  it into a neutral rating.
- **Satisfaction and importance are `-` unless she rated them.** She did not, so
  they are marked not-asked, not inferred from tone.
- **Key Insights holds only what changed someone's mind.** The
  just-back-from-service gap qualifies — the team's model of the problem was
  "wrong ranking", and this says "missing input data". The rewriting does not
  qualify; it was already known.
- **Action items are dated and owned**, with the owner drawn from Mentix's team,
  not the upstream template's example name.
- Any inference the summariser adds is marked as theirs.

## Auto-fail if

Records "it's fine, better than nothing" as satisfaction; assigns a numeric
satisfaction or importance score she did not give; puts the paper list under
Problems rather than recognising it as her current solution; fills Key Insights
with restatement; leaves the upstream example owner name in an action item;
invents a date, a role, or a quote; or adds a Problems line the transcript does
not support.
