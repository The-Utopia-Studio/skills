I shouldn't write this script, and the reason is in your own sentence: *she
can't explain how she decides.*

Asking questions is the wrong instrument for knowledge the holder can't
articulate. She'll answer — in good faith — with a plausible post-hoc rule that
sounds clean and won't be what she actually does. And the gap between the stated
rule and the real behaviour will be widest exactly at the edge cases, which are
the whole value of twenty years.

**Use `tacit-knowledge-interview` instead.** It shadows the expert doing real
work: watch her review applications for 30 minutes, replay the decisions she
made for 20, then spend 10 on the edges where she overrode her own rule. Output
is a Tacit-Knowledge Capture — the explicit procedure plus the judgment calls
that don't fit it.

The boundary between the two skills: this one asks people about their
experience; that one watches them and asks about what just happened.
Articulable knowledge answers questions. Tacit knowledge doesn't.

**Where I come back in.** Once the shadowing produces an explicit flagging
procedure, that rule needs testing against the *other* reviewers — does it
generalise, or is it hers? Those are ordinary interviews with a concrete thing
to ask about, and I'll script them.
