# Booth intercept — 10 minutes, standing, no recording

Ten minutes standing up isn't a short version of a 30-minute seated interview.
It's a different instrument, so most of the standard script is cut rather than
compressed.

**What's cut and why:** the warm-up (no rapport time to spend), the probing
sequence (no room to ask "why" three times), and most of the wrap-up. A
two-minute version of a twenty-minute JTBD exploration isn't a small one — it's
a different question.

**What survives:** one past-tense specific-instance question, one follow-up, and
one closing ask.

## The script

**Open (20 sec)**
- "Mind if I ask you one thing about how you work? I'm not selling anything."

**The spine (6-7 min) — one question, then follow the story**
- "Walk me through the last time you had to [do the thing]. What happened?"

Then, whichever fits what they just said:
- "What was the hardest part of that?"
- "What did you use to do it?"
- "How long did that take?"
- "What did you try before that?"

**Close (1 min)**
- "Have you found anything that helps?"
- "Can I follow up properly by email?" *(a booked follow-up is worth more than
  the intercept)*

## Note card — fill it before the next person

```
ID: [ ]  Role: [ ]  Company type: [ ]
LAST INSTANCE (what they actually did): [ ]
HARDEST PART (their words): [ ]
CURRENT METHOD / TOOL: [ ]
FOLLOW-UP AGREED? [y/n]  Contact: [ ]
```

Four fields, fillable standing up. No impressions field — at a booth, after
twenty conversations, impressions are the only thing you'll remember and the
least reliable thing you'll have.

## What this cannot deliver

People who walk up to a vendor booth selected themselves. That is not a sample,
and thirty intercepts is not thirty interviews. Whatever comes out is
hypothesis-generation — a list of things worth asking properly later. If it gets
reported as "we validated X at the show", the number will do damage
disproportionate to how it was collected.

The booked follow-ups are the real output. Aim for those.
