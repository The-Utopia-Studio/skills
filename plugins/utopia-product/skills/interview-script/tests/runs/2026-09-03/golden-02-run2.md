# Diagnosis — why the interviews go well and teach you nothing

Diagnose mode. I'm not writing you a fresh script yet; the questions you have
are the evidence.

## 1. The mechanism

Your interviews aren't failing, they're going *pleasantly* — and that's a
predictable output of describing the idea and then asking for a reaction.

The moment your idea is on the table, the person's job changes. They stop
reporting their own life and start evaluating your proposal. Evaluating a
stranger's proposal politely is what nearly everyone does. So "sounds great" is
a fact about human manners, not about your product. The non-signups aren't a
contradiction of the calls — they're the same data point read honestly.

"Compliments are noise" is the rule. The mechanism is why, and it's the part
that lets you catch this yourself next time.

## 2. Question by question

I need your actual script to do this properly, but the three lines you quoted
already fail on the same two tests:

| What they said back | What was asked | Past-tense? | Specific instance? | Mentions your product? | Answerable politely? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "sounds great" | something describing the idea | no | no | yes | yes |
| "they'd definitely use it" | a would-you question | no | no | yes | yes |
| "they love the idea" | a reaction request | no | no | yes | yes |

Any question that mentions your product **or** can be answered politely is
deleted, not softened. A well-phrased leading question is a more effective
leading question. Expect this to remove most of your script — that's the fix,
not a setback.

The replacements are the same three, inverted:

- "Walk me through the last time you had to [do the thing]. What happened?"
- "What have you already tried to make that easier? What happened to it?"
- "How long did that take, and what did it stop you doing?"

## 3. What's missing

There's no disconfirming answer written down. Put one line at the top of the
script: *what would I hear that means I'm wrong?* If you can't write it, the
interview has no way to come back negative — and then the compliments aren't a
bad outcome, they're the only available outcome.

## 4. What won't fix it

More interviews. A different segment. A better closer.

The script is the variable. Twenty more calls with this script return twenty
more compliments, and you'll have spent twenty slots confirming manners.

## 5. The signal you're missing

An existing workaround. A spreadsheet someone maintains, a rule in their mail
client, a recurring calendar block, a person they ping when it goes wrong.
Someone maintaining a workaround has already paid for this problem with their
own time. Someone saying "sounds great" has paid nothing.

One workaround outweighs ten enthusiastic calls — and no opinion question can
surface it, which is why your current script has never found one.
