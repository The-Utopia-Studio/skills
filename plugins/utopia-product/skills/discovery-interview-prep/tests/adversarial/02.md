# Adversarial 02 — Solution in disguise

## Input

"Plan interviews to find out if users want our new dashboard redesign. We'll
show them the mockups and ask if they'd prefer it to the current version, and if
they'd pay more for it."

## Required behaviour

Do not accept the framing. Three separate errors, and they compound.

**"Do users want X" is not a discovery question.** It is a preference question
about a future artifact, and the answers are worthless — the skill's own
Anti-Patterns say *"Discovery = learning problems; testing = validating
solutions"*.

**Showing mockups makes this a different study.** Once the mockup is on screen,
the session is reaction to a stimulus, not discovery. If reaction to the mockup
is genuinely the goal, this is `usability-test-protocol` (can they use it) or
`first-mocks` / `paper-sketch-probe` (is it legible), not an interview plan.

**"Would you pay more" is the weakest question in research.** Stated
willingness-to-pay about a hypothetical, asked by the person who built it, of
someone being shown a nice picture. It predicts nothing. Price research needs a
different instrument and a different sample.

A passing response:
- Names all three: preference-not-problem, stimulus-contaminates-discovery, and
  hypothetical-WTP-is-worthless.
- Asks what decision this actually informs. If it is "should we ship the
  redesign", the honest instruments are usability testing on the mockups plus
  behavioural evidence on the current version — not an interview asking for a
  verdict.
- Reframes to a question that can come back negative: what are users failing to
  do on the current dashboard, where do they stall, and what do they do instead?
  That is discoverable and it is what should have preceded the redesign.
- Routes the price question out: real willingness-to-pay comes from what people
  already spend and what they have already switched for, not from an opinion in
  a mockup review. Routes to `value-based-pricing`.
- Invents no users, no mockup details, no price points.

## Fail if

It plans the preference study as asked; keeps "would you prefer it" or "would
you pay more" as research questions; treats stated WTP as usable evidence;
invents user segments or price sensitivity; or fails to name that mockups turn
discovery into testing.
