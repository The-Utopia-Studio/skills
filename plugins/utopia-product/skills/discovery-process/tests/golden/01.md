# Golden 01 — Retention drop, one bounded cycle (typical)

## Input

Azraq. "Retention on the operator dashboard dropped 18 points last quarter —
from 71% to 53% on 30-day. Nobody knows why. We have four weeks before roadmap
planning. Run us through a discovery cycle."

## Expected shape of a good output

- Opens as a **bounded cycle with an end date**, mapping the 6 phases onto the
  four weeks, and states the end state up front: this ends in build, kill, or
  keep-learning — not in a document.
- Adopts `workshop-facilitation` as the interaction protocol; starts at
  **Phase 1 (Frame the Problem)** and stops for the fellow rather than
  pre-running the cycle.
- Treats the 18-point drop as the **symptom, not the problem**. Phase 1 frames
  who is dropping and what they were trying to do; it does not name a cause.
- Names the **three decision points** as real gates with real exit criteria, not
  checkpoints: DP1 enough context to start research, DP2 saturation reached,
  DP3 did the experiments validate. Says what "no" looks like at each.
- **Phase 2 routes out for the research design**, to `discovery-interview-prep`
  (who, how many, which method) and then `interview-script` (the questions).
  It does not re-derive them inline.
- Notes the cheapest signal first: the product is live and retention is
  measured, so the cohort data and session behaviour already hold part of the
  answer. Routes to `trace-to-interview` for the recorded-behaviour half before
  spending scarce interview slots.
- **Invents no causes.** Pricing, onboarding, a competitor launch — naming any
  of these now would pre-empt the research the cycle exists to run.

## Auto-fail if

Proposes hypotheses for the retention drop as though they were findings; runs
all 6 phases in one response; skips or softens a decision point; produces a
"discovery report" rather than a decision; invents cohort numbers, interview
quotes, or competitor moves; or routes to `continuous-discovery-engine` (the
fellow asked for a bounded investigation with a deadline, not a standing
practice).
