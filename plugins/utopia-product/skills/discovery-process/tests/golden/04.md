# Golden 04 — Well-understood problem (mundane; is a cycle warranted?)

## Input

A fellow. "Run a discovery cycle on adding SSO. Every enterprise prospect asks
for it, our three biggest customers have asked, and sales has lost two deals
over it."

## Expected shape of a good output

The mundane case tests whether the skill will say no when the problem is already
solved.

- Names it honestly: this problem is **already validated by behaviour and
  money** — two lost deals is the strongest evidence available, and three named
  customers asking is behaviour, not opinion. A 3-4 week cycle to confirm what
  lost revenue already proved is a delay, not diligence.
- Applies the skill's own exclusion: *"For well-understood problems (move to
  execution)."*
- Says what is *actually* still open, because it is not nothing: SSO is a family
  of decisions, not one feature — SAML vs OIDC, SCIM provisioning or not, which
  identity providers first, whether it gates a pricing tier. That is scoping
  work, and it routes to `create-prd` or `one-pager-prd` depending on whether
  the fellow needs a spec or an approval.
- If any research is warranted it is narrow and named: asking the two lost deals
  what specifically blocked them, which is one or two conversations, not a
  cycle.
- Does not inflate a validated problem into a discovery project to justify
  process.

## Auto-fail if

Runs the cycle; treats "enterprise prospects ask for it" as an unvalidated
assumption needing interviews; invents deal values or prospect names; fails to
name the scoping decisions that are genuinely open; or refuses without saying
where to go next.
