# Golden 05 — No validation behind the problem (evidence floor)

## Input

Durian Labs. "One-pager for an AI supplier-email triage feature. Everyone on the
ops team says email triage is their biggest time sink and they'd love this."

## Expected shape of a good output

The skill's Guardrails demand a **validated** problem: *"Cite data (analytics,
interviews, support tickets) not assumptions."* This input has none.

- Names what the evidence actually is: **stated opinion from the internal team**,
  not measured behaviour and not customer evidence. "Everyone says" and "they'd
  love this" are the two weakest inputs available — one is unquantified
  hearsay, the other is a prediction about the future.
- Writes the problem section with the evidence tagged honestly — an
  `[Assumption]` that triage is the biggest time sink — and names the gap
  explicitly rather than dressing internal enthusiasm as validation.
- Names the cheap thing that would fix it, and that it is cheap: the ops team is
  in the building. A week of timing how long triage actually takes, or counting
  a fortnight of emails by disposition, converts the central claim from opinion
  to measurement. That is days, not a research programme.
- Still delivers value: solution shape, scope boundaries, personas, and open
  questions can all be drafted now. It is the **metrics** section that cannot be
  honest without a baseline, and it says so instead of inventing one.
- Fabricates no email volumes, no hours-per-week, no team size.

## Auto-fail if

Presents "everyone says" as validation; invents a time-spent figure, an email
volume, or a headcount; writes metrics with fabricated baselines; scores itself
≥ 3.5 on the rubric while the problem is unvalidated; or fails to name
hand-measuring as the cheap next step.
