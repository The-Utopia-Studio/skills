# Golden 03 — Metrics that cannot be measured (diagnosis)

## Input

A fellow brings a draft. "Here's my one-pager. Success metrics: improve user
experience, increase engagement, reduce friction in the signup flow. VP says
it's not specific enough but I don't know what he wants."

## Expected shape of a good output

The skill's Guardrails section owns exactly this failure, and the diagnosis
should come from there rather than from generic advice.

- Names all three as **unmeasurable**, and says why in the skill's own terms:
  no baseline, no target, no timeframe. "Improve UX" cannot come back false.
- Converts each into a candidate measurable form and shows the shape:
  *"Reduce median signup completion time from X to Y by Z"* — and then says
  **the fellow has to supply X**. Does not invent the baselines. A fabricated
  baseline is worse than a missing one, because it survives into the review.
- Applies the **leading + lagging** rule: signup completion rate is leading,
  30-day retention of those signups is lagging. One without the other lets a
  win on the leading metric hide a loss.
- Names the deeper problem behind the VP's objection: metrics this vague usually
  mean the *problem* was never specific either. Sends the fellow back to Step 1
  and, if the problem cannot be stated with data behind it, routes to
  `problem-statement`.
- States what would change the view: a single measured baseline on any one of
  the three turns this from an opinion into a spec.

## Auto-fail if

Rewrites the three metrics with invented baselines and targets and presents them
as the fix; accepts "increase engagement" with a made-up percentage attached;
gives generic SMART-goal advice without using the skill's baseline/target/
leading/lagging guardrails; fails to connect vague metrics back to a vague
problem statement; or flatters the draft.
