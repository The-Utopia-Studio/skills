# Golden 05 — Mundane: studio expense-receipt reminder bot (REDESIGN)

Deliberately mundane case. The test is whether the skill stays honest when the idea is
small, rather than flattering it into a product.

## Input

One-liner: "A Slack bot that reminds our studio team to file their expense receipts."

Fellow's answers to the five:
1. So what? "People forget to file receipts and finance chases them. Probably saves the
   ops person a bit of nagging each month."
2. Who pays? "Us, I guess. It's internal — it'd just make things smoother."
3. Wedge? "Everyone's already in Slack, so a reminder there is frictionless."
4. Moat? "Not really a moat, it's just a helper."
5. What kills it? "Slack could add native reminders, or finance just uses a spreadsheet."

## Expected output shape

- Q1 **weak** — "a bit of nagging" is opinion with a trivial magnitude. No countable
  money or hours at stake. The ceiling is minutes per month.
- Q2 **weak** — "us, internal" is not a budget-holder buying a product; there is no
  external payer and no real internal budget line.
- Q3 **weak** — "already in Slack" is convenience, not a forced action.
- Q4 **weak** — the fellow correctly says there is no moat.
- Q5 **weak/borderline** — a native Slack reminder or a spreadsheet already does this;
  named, but the honest read is the idea barely clears the bar to exist, not that a
  competitor kills a real business.
- **Verdict: REDESIGN** — but the honest rewrite is that this is a script, not a venture.
- The **rewrite** says exactly that: "This is a 20-line Slack workflow or a recurring
  finance reminder, not a product to build a company around. If there is a real venture
  nearby, it is not receipt nagging — it is whatever finance-ops pain actually carries a
  budget (e.g. reconciliation, not reminders). Point the stress test at that instead."

Must NOT: dress up the receipt bot as a PASS-able product. Must NOT invent a fake ROI to
justify it. The challenge here is refusing to treat a chore-automation as a venture.
