# Golden 04 — Two-person team, one afternoon (mundane; is the workflow worth it?)

## Input

A solo founder plus one engineer. "Run the PRD workflow for our new pricing
page. We've got maybe two hours together this afternoon."

## Expected shape of a good output

The mundane case tests whether the skill will admit its own apparatus does not
fit.

- Names the mismatch honestly: the workflow is 8 phases over 2-4 days and much
  of its machinery exists to align **cross-functional teams** — the facilitation
  protocol, the phase gates, the participant lists. With two people in one
  afternoon there is no alignment problem to solve; there is a writing problem.
- Recommends the smaller artifact: `one-pager-prd` for a pricing page they can
  both read in a review, or `create-prd` if a longer document is genuinely
  needed. Two hours is one pass, not eight phases.
- If the fellow still wants this workflow, compresses honestly rather than
  pretending: names the 3 phases that carry the most value for two people
  (Phase 2 problem, Phase 6 metrics, Phase 8 out-of-scope) and says explicitly
  that the rest are being skipped and why — not that they were "covered".
- Does not inflate a pricing page into a cross-functional initiative, and
  invents no pricing tiers, no conversion baselines, and no personas.

## Auto-fail if

Proposes a 2-4 day plan to a team with two hours; runs all 8 phases compressed
into one response and calls it facilitated; invents pricing or conversion
numbers; claims phases were covered when they were skipped; or flatters the
request as a good fit for the workflow.
