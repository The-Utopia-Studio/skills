# Growth Strategy Playbook

Where this came from and why it's shaped the way it is, for whoever maintains this skill next.

## Origin

Built from the first real growth strategy done this way: Pocket Health, August 2026. Two things happened worth remembering:

1. The first draft answered the right questions but used a generic navy/gold corporate template, because nobody had actually pulled the studio's real design system. It took a second pass, and the fellow saying plainly "this is not close to my kind of structure," before the real brand system got used. Don't repeat that: pull the real tokens before building (see SKILL.md, Brand System).
2. The first draft also read as AI-generated: em dashes throughout, one metaphor (Stripe) reused mechanically in almost every section, and some generic corporate phrasing. The fix was straightforward once named: ban em dashes outright, use an analogy once and only where it earns its place, and match the plain, confident register of the fellow's own existing materials rather than a generic "consulting deck" voice.

## Why this structure

The six subsections map to the questions a studio partner actually needs answered to decide whether and how to back a fellow's growth:

- **Value proposition** -- is this a real, sellable thing, described in plain terms.
- **Target segments, with named accounts** -- not "the market," but specific companies with specific reasons, because a growth strategy that can't name its first five calls isn't actually a plan yet. The Qatar-specific table exists because Utopia Studio is Doha-based (see `utopia-partnership-deck`'s "Why Doha" section) -- every fellow should have a considered point of view on the Gulf angle, even if the honest answer for some fellow is "not yet, and here's why."
- **Business model** -- who actually pays, and through what mechanism, since "enterprise + eventual marketplace" is a different plan than "pure marketplace from day one."
- **Collaboration tiers** -- how a real sales conversation actually escalates, lightest commitment first. This is what turns "we'll sell to insurers" into something a partnerships or BD person can execute against.
- **Growth flywheel** -- forces an honest answer to "does this get structurally better with use, or does it just get bigger." Not every fellow has a real second loop (e.g. cross-border distribution through a shared partner) -- don't invent one if it isn't there.
- **6-month GTM plan** -- the only section with a clock on it. Solutions-led-first is the default because it's usually true: you need proof before a platform-led/self-serve motion has anything to point to. Call out explicitly when a market can't support a platform-led motion yet (e.g., no public dataset to build a self-serve tool on) rather than defaulting every market to the same plan.

## Research checklist (expand or trim per fellow)

- [ ] Read every existing internal doc on this fellow (exec summary, MVP/pilot plans, prior board material) before writing a word.
- [ ] For each named target-market table, confirm: regulatory regime (is coverage/purchase of the fellow's category mandated, licensed, or informal), the 3-5 real named players who'd plausibly be a first call, and whether the primary market's data/infrastructure assumptions hold there.
- [ ] For every named account, one sentence of "why them, why now" that would survive the fellow's own team reading it and saying "yes, that's accurate."
- [ ] For competitors mentioned, confirm what they actually do and where the gap is -- don't assert a gap without checking.

## Example: a market that structurally differs from the fellow's home market

Pocket Health's UK/US MVP leans on public price-and-quality datasets (PHIN/CQC in the UK, price-transparency files in the US) to launch a self-serve pilot with zero data-sharing agreement. Qatar has no equivalent public dataset -- health cover there is mandatory under Law No. 22 of 2021 and licensed through the Qatar Central Bank, but the actual price/quality data sits inside insurers and providers, not in a public register. That single fact changes the whole Qatar GTM motion from "public-data pilot" to "must start as a direct data partnership." This is the kind of structural difference the research step exists to catch -- don't assume a new market behaves like the first one.

## QA before delivering

Beyond the docx skill's general render-and-look QA:

- Search the rendered text for `—` (em dash) and for any leftover `[bracketed placeholder]`. Either one means it's not done.
- Read section 1.5 (Growth Flywheel) and ask: is the second loop, if present, actually true for this fellow, or was it copied from the template's shape without being re-derived? If in doubt, cut it rather than leave a hollow paragraph.
- Read the two named-account tables aloud. If any "Why" cell could be pasted into a different fellow's document unchanged, it's too generic -- make it specific to this fellow's actual relationship or opportunity with that account.

## Should this be a skill?

Yes -- this is exactly the case: a recurring, structurally repeatable deliverable with a real house style, previously re-explained (and re-derived, and initially gotten wrong) from scratch each time. Packaging it means the brand lesson from Pocket Health's first draft doesn't have to be relearned by whoever builds the next fellow's growth strategy.
