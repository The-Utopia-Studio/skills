# Copy rules

A one pager is read in about ninety seconds by someone who is deciding whether to take a meeting. Every line has to earn its place, and the failure mode is not bad writing, it is writing that could have been about any company in the category.

## The register

- **Declarative, short, specific.** `A gas release starts a clock.` Five words, and the reader already understands the product.
- **Mechanism over category.** Never `AI-powered platform for X`. Say what the software reads, what it derives, and what it hands back.
- **Concrete nouns over abstractions.** `meters, allocation systems, operator ERPs`, not `enterprise data sources`.
- **Say the limit out loud.** `Never in the trip path.` `No production data.` `Read-only.` Stating what the product will not do is the fastest way to be believed about what it does.
- **No em dashes.** Use `·`, a comma, a colon, or a full stop. This is a hard rule for studio output and the `·` is already the format's native separator.
- **No filler.** Strike `in today's landscape`, `it's not just X, it's Y`, `leverage`, `seamless`, `revolutionary`, `cutting-edge`, `delve`.
- **No unearned superlatives.** `No VC-funded competitor found.` is a checkable claim. `The market leader` is not.
- **Vary construction.** The biggest tell of a templated page is four numbered points that all open with the same grammar.

Match the company's own voice where it already exists, in their manifesto or site copy. The formats above are a container, not a house style that overrides theirs.

## Fixed furniture

Some strings are part of the format and should survive unless there is a real reason to change them.

| Slot | Convention |
|---|---|
| Header pill | `One Page Brief` |
| Section titles, Type 1 | `The challenge` · `The solution` · `Differentiation` · `The asks` |
| Section eyebrows, Type 1 | A short scope line: `Every encrypted system, every day` · `Why this works, and why now` · `Where an introduction changes the outcome` |
| Column eyebrows, Type 2 | `01 · THE PROBLEM` · `02 · THE SOLUTION` · `03 · WHAT'S DIFFERENT`, mono uppercase |
| Stakes line | `The stakes:` then ` · ` separated consequences |
| Standing line | `Standing:` then ` · ` separated verifiable facts |
| Logo disclaimer | `Target organisations shown for introduction context; no partnership implied.` |
| Closing tagline | One sentence, imperative or declarative, carrying the manifesto |

The numbering (`01`, `02`, `03`, `04`) is load-bearing. It tells the reader the page is an argument in order, not a collection of panels.

## Sourcing claims

Every number on the page carries its source inline, in muted type, immediately after the claim:

- `3,712 alarms in 12 h before two died, CSB 2024`
- `a jet fire ruptures a 2-tonne vessel in 3.5 to 5 minutes (IChemE 2000)`
- `36 to 69% of releases missed (HSE RR672)`
- `Deepwater Horizon, USCG/BOEMRE 2011`

If you cannot source a number, cut it. A qualitative sentence with no number is stronger than a number the reader cannot check, because the second one invites them to check it and find nothing.

Product screenshots carry their own provenance note: `Our platform, August 2026 build. Modelled, anonymised module.` Never present a mockup as a live system, and never show real customer data without saying it is anonymised.

## Section by section

### 01 The challenge

The headline is the whole page. It should assert a gap that exists right now and that the reader has not framed this way before. Two sentences at most, and the second clause carries the accent colour.

Structure that works in both examples: name the clock, then name what fails to measure it.

> A gas release starts a clock. Today's fire and gas systems run it out.
> Quantum risk starts before the break. Most security stacks cannot see the migration window.

Then the body paragraph widens it, and ends on a bolded reframe: `The question is not only what algorithm is deployed. It is how the system behaves while you move.`

The three cause cards are the failure modes, each with a mono uppercase label that names the mode (`01 SILENCED ON PURPOSE`, `02 PHYSICALLY MISSED`, `03 DROWNED IN NOISE`), a plain-English title, a two-line explanation and a source. Three distinct causes, not three restatements of one.

### 02 The solution

Open with where the software sits, because that is the first objection: `Software beside your fire and gas system, never in the trip path.` `Software beside your cryptographic stack, without decrypting the payload.`

Then the four points. Give each a different job: what it measures, what it reveals, what it changes operationally, what it costs. Do not write four features.

The flow line is the deployment in one breath, arrow-separated, ending in the output. Keep it to four steps.

### 03 Differentiation

Four columns, and each one should answer a different objection a sceptic would actually raise:

1. Why is this framing right and the obvious alternative wrong (`Speed, not coverage`)
2. Why is the output trustworthy (`Physics signs the credit`)
3. Why now rather than five years ago (`Nobody at the panel`)
4. Why is the commercial shape acceptable (`Per asset, inside your perimeter`)

Then `Standing:` with the flat evidence list. Prototype status, test counts, verification against real data, filings, papers, acceptances. No adjectives at all in this line.

### 04 The asks

Two asks, at most three. Each names: what you want, who from, what it costs them, how long it takes, and how it ends. The ending is the part people forget and it is the part that gets the meeting:

> One unit, its existing study pack, a few weeks, no site access. If it shows nothing new, we stop.

The headline over the asks states the shape of the request and its outcome: `Two doors in Qatar. Each ends in a signed reference.`

### The founder panel

Six credentials, each of a different kind: practice, publication, standards, research, commercial, principle. Avoid six versions of "experienced in X". Every one should be independently checkable: a named committee, a named paper number, a named filing, a named employer with years.

The role sits in accent beside the name. The photo is a portrait, not a logo, not a group shot, not a conference stage wide shot.

### Type 2's founder line

Same job in one paragraph. Name, title, then the credential run: years, named employers, degrees with institution and year, then geography. Dense and checkable, no adjectives.

## Before and after

**Challenge headline**

> Before: Leveraging AI to transform industrial safety monitoring for the modern enterprise.
> After: A gas release starts a clock. Today's fire and gas systems run it out.

**Solution lead**

> Before: Our platform seamlessly integrates with your existing infrastructure to deliver actionable insights.
> After: Software beside your fire and gas system, never in the trip path. It measures the time to act you really have and shows the operator where to look first.

**Differentiation point**

> Before: Best-in-class accuracy powered by advanced physics simulation.
> After: Physics signs the credit. Virtual mesh adds awareness. Only physical detection earns credit. Never in the trip path.

**An ask**

> Before: We are seeking pilot partners and strategic introductions in the region.
> After: An operator site for a scoped pilot. One unit, its existing study pack, a few weeks, no site access. If it shows nothing new, we stop.

**Standing**

> Before: Significant traction with strong technical validation and IP protection.
> After: functional prototype · 139 engine tests green · real Gulf gas plant verified, anonymised · patent application filed · ADIPEC 2026 paper accepted

## Checklist before rendering

- Every number has a source beside it, or it is gone.
- The headline names a mechanism, not a category.
- The four solution points do four different jobs.
- The four differentiation points answer four different objections.
- Each ask says how it ends.
- Six credentials are six kinds of credential.
- `Standing:` contains zero adjectives.
- No em dash anywhere in the file.
- No bracketed placeholder, no `TODO`, no lorem.
- Read the closing tagline on its own. If it could belong to a competitor, rewrite it.
