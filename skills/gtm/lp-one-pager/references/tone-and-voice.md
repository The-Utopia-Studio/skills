# Tone and voice

## The persona

Write as **an investment professional explaining a technical company to other investment professionals.** Not the founder, not a marketer, not an engineer. Someone who has done the diligence, understands the mechanism properly, and now has ninety seconds to make a busy partner understand why the money is going somewhere.

That person has three habits worth copying:

1. **They lead with the money.** Not the technology, not the market size. What is being lost today, what the fix is worth, and who pays.
2. **They explain complexity by mapping it onto something the reader already owns.** A clearing house. An escrow. A custody chain. An audit trail. One good analogy does more work than a paragraph of description.
3. **They never oversell, because their credibility is the product.** Every claim is sized, dated and attributable. Where something is not known, they say so and move on.

The register is **confident, plain and quantified**. Think a strong IC memo read aloud: no hedging, no hype, no jargon left undefined.

## The value proposition, in four moves

A reader who does not understand the product cannot judge the position, so the page earns the right to talk about ownership and IRR by first making the business obvious. Four moves, in order:

**1. Name the loss.** Open the problem with what it costs, in money, time or exposure. "Disputed entitlements sit open for months" beats "reconciliation is inefficient."

**2. Give the analogy.** One sentence that maps the product onto a familiar financial object. "A clearing layer for the entitlement number." "Escrow for the measurement itself." "An audit trail that writes itself at close." Use it once, near the top, then drop it and speak plainly.

**3. Show the mechanism as steps.** Three numbered steps, each a verb. Reads, re-derives, proves. Connects, compares, reports. A mechanism read as three steps is understood; the same mechanism read as a paragraph is skimmed.

**4. Say what it replaces.** The clearest statement of value is displacement. "Replaces the reconciliation cycle." "Replaces the annual study." "Replaces a sampled look-back with a continuous record." A buyer who can see what falls out of their budget understands the price.

## Rules

- **Quantify or cut.** Any adjective doing the work a number should do is a defect. "Significant delay" out, "eleven weeks to close" in.
- **Date every figure.** A number without a period is not a number.
- **Define jargon inline, once, in three words.** `PSC · production sharing contract`, `allocation · splitting metered volume between owners`. Never make the reader look something up, and never explain the same term twice.
- **One analogy per page.** Two competing metaphors leave the reader with neither.
- **No stacked qualifiers.** "Potentially transformative" and "could meaningfully improve" both read as "we are not sure".
- **Prefer the concrete noun.** Meters, terminals, lifting statements, close. Not "data sources", "enterprise workflows", "operational touchpoints".
- **Active voice, present tense** for what is true now; **dated future tense** for what is planned. Never blur the two.
- **Short sentences carry numbers better.** If a sentence has two figures in it, it usually wants to be two sentences.
- **No em dashes.** Use `·`, a comma, a colon or a full stop.
- **Honour the brand's banned words.** The extractor prints them; the builder warns when one reaches the page.

## Words this register does not use

`revolutionary` · `cutting-edge` · `game-changing` · `seamless` · `robust` · `leverage` as a verb · `unlock` · `empower` · `solution` used alone as a noun for a product · `innovative` · `best-in-class` · `disruptive` · `synergy` · `ecosystem` where you mean customers · `journey` where you mean process

## Before and after

**Problem lead**

> Before: Reconciliation in multi-party oil and gas is inefficient and error-prone.
> After: One number settles the barrel, and only one party can compute it.

**Solution lead**

> Before: An AI-powered platform that streamlines fiscal verification workflows.
> After: A clearing layer for the entitlement number: computed once, checkable by every counterparty.

**Mechanism**

> Before: The system integrates with existing infrastructure to provide continuous, automated verification and reporting capabilities.
> After: **Reads.** Connects read-only to meters, allocation systems and operator ERPs. **Re-derives.** Recomputes the result under the terms already signed. **Proves.** Stamps a record every party can check at close.

**Thesis claim**

> Before: Strong founder with deep domain expertise in the target market.
> After: Forty years on the operator side of the table. The control library is written from a seat a software team cannot reconstruct from documentation.

**Displacement**

> Before: Significant cost savings and efficiency gains for customers.
> After: Replaces the reconciliation cycle. Priced per asset, against a study the operator already budgets for.

## A note on charts and figures

The same discipline applies to anything with a number in it. A donut whose segments are not labelled with their values is decoration. A bar chart of an allocation nobody has agreed is a liability. If a chart cannot carry its own provenance chip honestly, it does not belong on the page.
