# The corpus

The collection at [manifestos.tech](https://www.manifestos.tech/), curated by
Nicholas Chua, framed as manifestos that "paint a vision for what the world
could be". Thirty-six pieces, 2006 to 2025, weighted heavily toward AI.

Use this to send a user to a precedent, or to check whether a claimed pattern is
actually in the source material. Pages drift: several of these have been
rewritten or removed since the collection indexed them, so read what is live
before citing it as an example.

## What the distribution tells you

- **Category:** AI dominates (roughly 22 of 36), then software, energy, biotech,
  defence, manufacturing, consumer. Energy and manufacturing entries lean
  heavily on the Historical Inevitability shape. AI entries lean on the Named
  Problem.
- **Era:** the older entries (Mozilla 2007, Tesla 2006, OpenAI 2015) are longer,
  more argumentative, and more willing to be provisional. The 2023 to 2025
  entries are shorter and more assertive, and several are a single screen of
  text. Compression is the trend.
- **Form:** most of these are not standalone documents. They are the homepage,
  the about page, or the first blog post. The manifesto is usually the front
  door, not an artifact filed away.

## Notable entries, with what each teaches

| Piece | Year | Teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Safe Superintelligence | 2024 | Maximum compression. ~230 words, and a third of them explain why the funding structure removes short-term pressure. The cost is the argument. |
| Obsidian / "File over app" (Steph Ango) | 2023 | The Philosophy form. ~520 words, argues from clay tablets to plain text, ends by conceding its own app will die. |
| Tesla, "The Secret Master Plan" | 2006 | The Staged Plan. Opens by conceding the obvious objection, then makes the sequence the argument. Also the best case for letting personality into the form. |
| Mozilla Manifesto | 2007 | The Principles Charter. Ten principles separated cleanly from a short list of commitments, with consistent grammar in each block. |
| The Browser Company, values essay | 2021 | The Personal Testament. Opens by admitting corporate values make the author uncomfortable, then runs five values through one family road trip. |
| Physical Intelligence, π0 | 2024 | Named Problem plus technical proof. Antithesis opening, Moravec's paradox as the hinge, then technique, then multiple entry points. |
| Valar Atomics | 2023 | Historical Inevitability. Coal, oil, then this, followed immediately by materials and sites so the sweep is paid off. |
| Hadrian | 2020 | Same shape at industrial scale: named factories, years, a thirty-day training claim. Specificity as credibility. |
| Thinking Machines Lab | 2025 | Belief-as-heading structure. Four claim headings that carry the argument on their own, ending on recruiting. |
| Anthropic | 2021 | How to hold risk and ambition in the same document without either cancelling the other. |
| Linear | 2019 | The shortest respectable company manifesto. ~520 words, names coordination overhead as the condition it opposes. |
| OpenAI, "Introducing OpenAI" | 2015 | A founding post that stays provisional and admits what it does not know. Reads differently now, which is itself instructive. |
| OpenAI Charter | 2018 | Commitments that constrain the author. Written to be quoted back at them. |
| Anduril | 2018 | Naming institutional decay rather than a competitor. |
| Cluely | 2025 | Deliberate provocation as positioning. Worth reading for how a document survives, or does not survive, being disliked. |

## Reading a precedent properly

When studying one of these before writing, extract exactly these six things and
nothing else. Do not absorb the voice, absorb the moves.

1. The load-bearing sentence.
2. The naming line, and how many times it repeats.
3. Where the cost is stated, and whether it is stated at all.
4. What condition is named as the adversary.
5. The section headings read in sequence, with nothing between them.
6. The single call to action, and who it is addressed to.
