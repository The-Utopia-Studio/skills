# Qualification & the Evidence Ledger

## Six qualification types — do not collapse into one score

A single "qualified/not qualified" score hides which specific thing is actually uncertain. Track these separately:

| Type | Question it answers | Resolved by |
|---|---|---|
| **Account qualification** | Is this a real account worth pursuing? | Pipeline stage 1–2 |
| **Buyer qualification** | Have we found someone with real authority? | Pipeline stage 3, confirmed by stage 6–7 |
| **Problem qualification** | Do they actually have the problem, in their own words? | Pipeline stage 7 |
| **Solution qualification** | Does what exists today actually answer it? | Pipeline stage 9 |
| **Pilot qualification** | Can they provide a real environment and agree to success criteria? | Pipeline stage 11–12 |
| **Commercial qualification** | Is there budget and urgency, not just technical interest? | Pipeline stage 15 |

An opportunity can be strong on one and weak on another — e.g., strongly problem-qualified and buyer-qualified but not yet pilot-qualified because there's no environment access. Report the specific gap, not a single blended number.

## The qualification rubric — a starting hypothesis, not a measured instrument

Template signals (replace weights and signals with your own domain's — these are illustrative, not universal):

| Signal | Points | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Named authority/sponsor confirmed | +3 | Without this, little downstream tends to move |
| Prospect states the problem unprompted | +3 | Stronger than agreeing when the vendor states it |
| Can name a real environment/system to test against | +2 | Separates "interested" from "can actually test this" |
| A real, dated trigger is present | +2 | Deadline, incident, mandate, funding event |
| No sponsor after two real conversations | −3 | Do not keep pursuing on enthusiasm alone |
| Wants a demo but won't discuss real test conditions | −2 | Route to an evidence-building motion, not pilot qualification |

**The rule that matters more than any weight:** *qualitative evidence can invalidate a numerical score.* A high score contradicted by what someone actually said in a real conversation loses to the conversation, every time. Example: a prospect scores 8 on the rubric but says outright "we have no budget for this category this year" — that sentence overrides the 8.

Recalibrate weights only after ~10 real, scored outcomes exist. Before that, every weight and every threshold is itself tagged `Hypothesis`.

## The Evidence Ledger

Every claim a GTM motion rests on, in one place, with these fields:

| Field | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The specific assertion, stated plainly |
| **Source** | Customer conversation / pilot evidence / technical evidence / market research / partner feedback / internal hypothesis |
| **Date** | When this evidence was gathered — stale evidence should look stale |
| **Account** | Which specific opportunity or account this came from, if any |
| **Role** | Which role (buyer, champion, evaluator, blocker) produced it |
| **Evidence strength** | One of the five tags: Known / Observed / Hypothesis / Experiment / Decision rule |
| **Contradictory evidence** | Anything on record that argues against this claim — required, even if the entry is "none found yet" |
| **Current interpretation** | What the operator currently believes, given both columns above |
| **Next experiment** | What would move this claim, if it's not already Known |
| **Decision affected** | What downstream decision actually depends on this claim being right |

**The `contradictory evidence` field is not optional and is the whole point.** A ledger where every row's contradictory-evidence field is empty is not evidence of a strong position — it's usually evidence no one looked. Actively ask, for each claim: what have we seen, or heard, that argues against this? Log it even when it doesn't change the current interpretation yet.
