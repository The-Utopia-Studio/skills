# Worked Examples

Five generic archetypes. None reference a specific company, product, or real conversation — adapt the pattern, don't copy the specifics.

---

### 1. Unpaid, academic-style validation pilot

**Stage:** Pilot execution, if environment access and success criteria are genuinely agreed — otherwise still in qualification. *Illustrative branch — verify actual status before acting on it; don't assume a pilot is further along than confirmed.*

**Branch applied:** "prospect can provide an environment and agrees to success criteria" → pilot qualification, if that's genuinely where things stand.

**Evidence it can produce:** `Experiment` outcome — whether the product produces interpretable, decision-useful evidence on one real (if unpaid, academic) environment, reviewed by one real stakeholder.

**Do not do:** treat this as a confirmed beachhead because it's the active pilot. An unpaid, academic-context engagement validates technical and analyst usefulness at most — it says nothing about a paying buyer's willingness to pay, because no budget conversation occurred by construction. See the branching logic's "unpaid pilot" and "technical success without commercial intent" rules.

---

### 2. Consultancy or strategic-partner introduction

**Stage:** Entry point → typically stalls before account qualification.

**Branch applied:** "partner offers introductions but cannot identify a specific buyer or use case" → not pipeline, logged as an introduction hypothesis.

**Evidence it can produce:** `Hypothesis` only — whether this partner is a channel, a buyer, or an implementation partner remains open until they name a real account.

**Next commitment to ask for:** not "let's see who you know" — specifically, "can you name one account where this fits an active engagement." If they can't, this is relationship-building, not GTM motion, yet.

---

### 3. Economic-buyer conversation with a recognized problem

**Stage:** First conversation → qualification.

**Branch applied:** score against the qualification rubric in real time. A named sponsor plus an unprompted, self-stated problem is already a strong signal before systems or environments are even discussed.

**Evidence it can produce:** `Observed` — the buyer and problem hypotheses are confirmed or corrected in their own words.

**Watch for:** a recurring objection across multiple such conversations. If the same objection appears three or more times, that's the "repeated rejection" branch firing — revisit positioning before increasing outbound volume, not after.

---

### 4. Failed / no-response prospect

**Stage:** Outreach → no first conversation reached.

**Branch applied:** none yet, individually — a single non-response carries no signal on its own. The "repeated rejection" branch only fires at volume.

**Evidence it can produce:** `Observed`, but only meaningful in aggregate — track it as a denominator, not a data point to react to individually.

**Do not do:** read one non-response as disproving the account or market hypothesis. Equally, don't quietly stop counting non-responses — they're what makes every later conversion rate meaningful.

---

### 5. Technically successful pilot, no commercial intent

**Stage:** Pilot evidence met → commercial proposal stalls before it starts.

**Branch applied:** "pilot succeeds technically but no budget/urgency emerges" → not commercial traction; capture the evidence, continue market validation elsewhere.

**Evidence it can produce:** `Known` upgrade for technical and analyst-usefulness claims. `Hypothesis`, unmoved, for buyer usefulness — that question was never actually tested here.

**Do not do:** count this pilot as a GTM win in any commercial sense, or let "it worked technically" quietly stand in for "someone will pay for it" in a later strategy document. This is the single most common place activity gets misreported as traction — flag it explicitly every time it happens.
