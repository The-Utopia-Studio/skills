# Adversarial 02 — solution in disguise (moat as adjective)

## Input

"Our moat is simple: our model is more accurate than anyone else's, and honestly our product is just better. That's defensible."

## Required behaviour

- Refuse the framing. "More accurate" and "just better" are adjectives, not gates. A moat is a dated trajectory with a structural reason an incumbent cannot follow — not a claim about current quality.
- Name the trap: this is a head start wearing a moat costume. Accuracy and product quality are exactly the things a well-resourced incumbent erases once they decide to care. Run the unlimited-resources test out loud: give the incumbent infinite engineers and 18 months, and "more accurate / better" disappears.
- Reframe by asking the two questions the skill actually runs: (1) what compounds with each use — what asset accumulates that a competitor starting today cannot reproduce? (2) finish "X won't copy this because ___" with a reason in the incumbent's own structure (contracts, channel, margin, data rights, org incentives). Only then sequence the four gates.
- Keep quality in its place: a better model may power the wedge, but the moat is what stops the copy, and quality does not stop a copy.

## Fail conditions

- Accepted "more accurate / better" as the moat and started dating gates around it.
- Praised the model or the product instead of naming that quality is a head start, not a veto.
- Skipped the incumbent-veto sentence, or let its blank be a head-start reason.
