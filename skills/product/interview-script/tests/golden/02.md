# Golden 02 — Fellow keeps getting compliments (diagnosis)

## Input

A fellow. "My interviews keep going well but I learn nothing. People say it
sounds great, they'd definitely use it, they love the idea. Then nobody signs
up. What am I doing wrong?"

## Expected shape of a good output

The symptom is diagnostic and the SKILL.md's Mom Test rules name the cause
exactly. This is a script-structure failure, and this skill owns it.

- Names the mechanism, not just the rule: the fellow is **describing the idea
  and then asking for a reaction**. Once the idea is on the table the
  respondent's job switches from reporting their life to evaluating a proposal,
  and evaluating a proposal politely is what everyone does. "Compliments are
  noise" — the SKILL.md's own line — is the observation; the cause is that the
  questions are about the idea at all.
- Walks the specific fixes, each concrete:
  - **Delete every question that mentions the product.** If a question cannot be
    asked without describing what you are building, it is a pitch.
  - **Convert future to past.** "Would you use this" → "what did you do the last
    time this came up".
  - **Convert opinion to cost.** "Is this a problem for you" → "how long did
    that take, and what did it stop you doing".
  - **Add the disconfirming answer to the top of the script** so there is
    something the interview can come back with that is not a compliment.
- Names the strongest single signal to look for instead: what they have already
  tried and abandoned. Someone who has hacked a spreadsheet together has proved
  the problem with their own time; someone who says "sounds great" has proved
  nothing.
- Connects the symptom to the outcome the fellow reported: enthusiasm followed by
  no sign-ups is the expected result of asking for opinions, not a surprise.
  That is what makes the diagnosis credible.
- **Does not** blame the sample or recommend more interviews. Twenty more
  interviews with the same script produce twenty more compliments.

## Auto-fail if

Recommends more interviews or a different segment as the fix; gives generic
"ask better questions" advice without naming pitching as the mechanism; invents
what the fellow's product is; rewrites their script with product-mentioning
questions still in it; or treats this as a `discovery-interview-prep` sampling
problem.
