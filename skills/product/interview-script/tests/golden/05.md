# Golden 05 — Interviewing about something that does not exist yet (evidence floor)

## Input

Durian Labs. "Script to interview procurement managers about our AI
supplier-triage tool. We haven't built it. We want to know if they'd want it and
what they'd pay."

## Expected shape of a good output

The request asks for the two question types the method rules out. The right
output does not soften that.

- Names both directly: **"would they want it"** is a future-tense opinion about
  a thing that does not exist, and **"what would they pay"** is stated
  willingness-to-pay for a hypothetical, asked by the seller. The Mom Test rules
  in this skill's own body forbid both. Answers to them are 0.1 evidence — worth
  approximately nothing, and worse than nothing because they feel like data.
- **Reframes to what is answerable now**, and this is the substance of the good
  output: procurement managers triage supplier email *today*, by some method,
  at some cost. That is a real present-tense behaviour with a real history.
  The script asks about the last week's email, what they did with it, how long
  it took, what went wrong, and what they have already tried to fix it.
- Names what that buys: if triage is genuinely painful the interviews will
  surface workarounds, time costs, and abandoned attempts — evidence that
  survives the fact that the product does not exist. If it is not painful, the
  interviews say so, which is the more valuable outcome and the one a
  "would you want it" script cannot produce.
- Handles the pricing question honestly: what they spend today on tooling and
  headcount for this job is answerable and useful. Routes forward to
  `value-based-pricing` and says stated WTP for a hypothetical is not an input
  to it.
- Writes the script. This is not a refusal case — the reframe makes it
  answerable, and a full script comes back.
- Invents no manufacturers, no email volumes, no price points.

## Auto-fail if

Includes "would you want this" or "what would you pay" as questions; describes
the unbuilt tool to the respondent; treats stated WTP as usable evidence;
invents volumes or prices; or refuses to produce a script instead of reframing
it.
