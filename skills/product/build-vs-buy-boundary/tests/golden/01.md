# Golden 01 — Azraq: what's ours vs the studio's on the risk benchmark

## Input

Azraq (data-centre infrastructure risk). "We're building the cross-operator risk benchmark. Every month-end an operator uploads incident-and-uptime logs and gets a report ranking their sites against an anonymised peer benchmark. What do we build, and what does Daedalus give us? I don't want to waste time on plumbing but I don't want to give away anything that matters."

Evidence available: wedge passed `wedge-five-questions` 5/5 [Fact]; 4 operators, cross-operator incident corpus is the accumulating asset [Fact]; data-rights clause not yet signed [Fact].

## Expected shape

- A boundary map that assigns each component to Platform / Venture / Buy, not a prose essay.
- **Platform (inherit):** router, eval harness runner, RAG plumbing, guardrail engine, observability, auth/deploy. Explicitly named as carrying no domain signal.
- **Venture (moat):** the cross-operator incident **corpus**, the domain risk-ranking **reasoning**, the golden cases, the flywheel signal, the operator-facing report interface (thin). The moat claim on the corpus scored ≥ 0.5 (behaviour 0.7 — deposits an asset a single-operator rival cannot reproduce) and tagged.
- **Buy:** base model weights, cloud, vector-DB product.
- The substrate/signal split shown at least on RAG (plumbing = Platform, corpus + domain relevance = Venture) and on eval (runner = Platform, golden cases = Venture).
- Moat test applied per component with the discriminator sentence.
- A note that the corpus is only a real Venture-moat if Azraq's right to keep it is signed — routes to `data-rights-clause`; until then the moat claim rests on an `[Assumption]`.
- Verdict `BOUNDARY DRAWN` with next step: take the Venture lane into `moat-design-canvas`.
- Must NOT let the fellow rebuild the eval harness or RAG plumbing; must NOT accept outsourcing the corpus.
