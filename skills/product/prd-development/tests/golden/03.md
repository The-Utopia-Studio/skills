# Golden 03 — Stakeholders already committed to the solution (diagnosis)

## Input

Azraq. "Run the PRD workflow for the risk dashboard. To be clear, the dashboard
is decided — leadership announced it to customers last month. I need the PRD to
document it so engineering can build."

## Expected shape of a good output

- Names the situation precisely: the solution is fixed, and it was fixed by
  announcement rather than by evidence. Phases 2 (Problem Statement) and 5
  (Solution Overview) are therefore not open questions — they are being
  back-filled.
- Does **not** refuse the work. Documenting a committed decision for engineering
  is legitimate and needed. It says which phases still do real work and which
  are theatre:
  - Real: Phase 6 (success metrics — nobody announced those), Phase 7 (user
    stories and acceptance criteria), Phase 8 (out of scope, dependencies,
    risks), Phase 10 (open questions).
  - Back-filled: Phases 2 and 5. They should be written as *"this is the
    announced solution and the problem it was announced against"*, not dressed
    up as a discovery finding.
- Names the cost of pretending otherwise: a PRD whose problem statement was
  reverse-engineered from the solution cannot be used to descope later, because
  every cut looks like a betrayal of the announcement.
- Flags the one genuine risk to surface in Phase 9: the announcement created an
  external commitment before scope existed. That is a dependency on
  customer expectation, and it belongs in the risk register in writing.
- Routes the strategic half honestly: if leadership wants to know whether the
  dashboard was the right call, that is not this workflow.

## Auto-fail if

Runs all 8 phases as though the solution were open, producing a discovery
narrative that ends at the pre-decided answer; refuses the work entirely; fails
to name which phases are back-filled; invents the problem the dashboard was
announced against; or omits the announcement from the risk register.
