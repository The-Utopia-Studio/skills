# Golden 01 — Mentix: where does a human sign off on the copilot

## Input

Mentix has an eval-first spec for its factory-manager copilot. The taxonomy names four
modes with costs: recommending a line **shutdown** wrongly (High — lost production, ~$40k/hr
`[Assumption]`), recommending a **reorder** of the job queue wrongly (Low — the manager
reshuffles, minutes lost), a **Confidently-wrong** root-cause label (High — sends the crew
to the wrong machine), and a **Wrong-format** digest (Low). Autonomy is L1 (copilot drafts,
manager acts). Fellow: "Where does a human need to sign off? My instinct is the manager
reviews everything the copilot says."

## Expected shape

- Places each mode on the matrix: shutdown recommendation = High × Low volume (shutdowns are
  rare) → Q3; reorder = Low × High → Q6; wrong root-cause = High × Medium/High → Q4;
  wrong-format = Low × High → Q6.
- Declines "reviews everything." Names it the uniform stack: on the Low-cost reorder and
  format modes a sign-off is waste and will be dropped in practice; the manager's attention
  should be rationed to the High-cost modes.
- Puts an explicit human sign-off trigger only on the shutdown recommendation (and below-τ
  root-cause calls): trigger, reviewer (the shift manager), SLA, fail-safe on timeout (the
  shutdown is NOT auto-suggested / is held).
- Low-cost modes get rules-in-code + a confidence log, no per-item human.
- Tags every cost `[Fact]`/`[Assumption]`; notes the shutdown $/hr is the number that makes
  that mode High and worth a sign-off.
- Does not invent an acceptable rate Mentix did not supply; if the shutdown mode has no
  cost-of-one-failure, routes back to eval-first-spec for it.
