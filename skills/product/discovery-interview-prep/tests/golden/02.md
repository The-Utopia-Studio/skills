# Golden 02 — Churn interviews, respondents are gone (edge)

## Input

Azraq. "We want to interview churned operators to find out why they left. 40
accounts churned last quarter. How do we run this?"

## Expected shape of a good output

- Names the defining problem of churn research immediately: **the people who
  churned are the hardest to reach, and the ones who answer are not
  representative.** Response rates on churned accounts are low and skewed —
  people who reply are disproportionately the angry and the loyal-but-blocked.
  The silent middle, which is usually most of the churn, does not answer.
- Builds the sample plan around that rather than ignoring it: over-recruit,
  segment the invite by churn reason if any is recorded, and state up front what
  the achieved sample will and will not represent.
- **Names what the plan cannot fix**: non-response bias here is not a
  methodological wrinkle, it is the main threat to the finding. The plan says
  so, and says what would triangulate it — the churn cohort's *behaviour* before
  they left is recorded and does not require anyone to answer an email. Routes
  to `trace-to-interview` for that half.
- **Distinguishes stated reason from actual cause.** Churn exit reasons are
  post-hoc rationalisations; people give the reason that is easiest to say.
  The method should ask about the last time they tried to do the job and what
  happened, not "why did you leave".
- Recommends a realistic target from 40 accounts and says the arithmetic
  honestly rather than asserting a number: at typical churned-account response
  rates a single-digit achieved sample is the expectation, so the plan should
  be designed for that, not for 15.
- Routes onward to `interview-script`.

## Auto-fail if

Assumes a response rate or an achieved sample size as if measured; treats stated
exit reasons as causes; omits non-response bias; proposes a survey to 40
churned accounts as the primary method without naming that it inherits the same
bias worse; invents churn reasons or account names; or writes the interview
questions.
