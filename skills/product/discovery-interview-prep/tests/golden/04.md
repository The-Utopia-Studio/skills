# Golden 04 — Three interviews to "validate" a whole product (mundane/undersized)

## Input

A fellow. "I've got three friends who are in our target market. Plan the
interviews so we can validate the product idea."

## Expected shape of a good output

Two problems, both worth naming plainly and without lecturing.

- **Three is below what the method can support**, and the SKILL.md's own
  Anti-Patterns set the qualitative band at 5-10. Three interviews can generate
  hypotheses; they cannot validate anything. Saying "validate" over n=3 is the
  error, not the sample size itself.
- **Friends are a biased sample in a specific, correctable way**: they want the
  fellow to succeed, so they will be generous, and generous is indistinguishable
  from positive in a transcript. The plan should name this as the dominant bias.
- Reframes the goal honestly rather than refusing: three friendly conversations
  are a legitimate **hypothesis-generation** exercise — surfacing what the job
  actually is, what language people use, what they do today. That is real value,
  and it is what n=3 supports. Validation comes later with strangers.
- Names the practical mitigation: ask them about the past and about specifics,
  which is harder to be generous about than an opinion. "Walk me through the
  last time" survives friendship; "would you use this" does not.
- Says what would upgrade the study: introductions from the three friends to
  people who do not know the fellow. That converts a biased n=3 into an
  unbiased n=6-9 for the cost of an ask, which is the highest-leverage move
  available here.
- Routes onward to `interview-script`.

## Auto-fail if

Plans it as a validation study; ignores or footnotes the friendship bias;
invents the target market or the product; claims n=3 can validate; refuses
without offering the hypothesis-generation reframe; or fails to name the
referral upgrade.
