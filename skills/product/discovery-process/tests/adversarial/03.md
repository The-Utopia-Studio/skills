# Adversarial 03 — Out of scope (belongs to a sibling)

## Input

"We built the prototype. Get 8 users in front of it and find out whether they
can complete the three main flows without help, and where they get stuck."

## Required behaviour

Decline and name the right skill. Do not poach.

This is usability testing, and it belongs to **`usability-test-protocol`**. The
skill's own Anti-Patterns section draws the line: *"Not user testing: Discovery
validates problems; testing validates solutions."*

Every tell is a testing tell: an existing prototype, task completion, a fixed
number of participants, and "where they get stuck". None of that is problem
discovery — the problem is settled and the artifact exists.

A passing response:
- Declines to open the cycle.
- Routes explicitly to `usability-test-protocol` and says what it does (task
  scenarios, observed completion, where attention breaks).
- Draws the boundary cleanly: this skill asks *is this the right thing to
  build*; that skill asks *can they use the thing we built*. Running a discovery
  cycle on a finished prototype answers neither — it re-opens a settled question
  while leaving the usability one untouched.
- Offers the in-scope handoff: if the sessions reveal that users do not have the
  problem the prototype solves — a common and expensive finding — that is when
  this skill's cycle becomes the right next step.
- Invents no flows, no participants, no failure points.

## Fail if

It runs a discovery cycle on the prototype; designs discovery interview
questions instead of task scenarios; guesses where users will get stuck; treats
this as a `first-mocks` or design-critique request; or declines without naming
`usability-test-protocol`. (Related sibling collisions to also decline
correctly: a standing discovery practice → `continuous-discovery-engine`; the
research plan alone → `discovery-interview-prep`; the questions alone →
`interview-script`; behaviour already in the logs → `trace-to-interview`.)
