# Golden 03 — Barrier Intelligence: "alerts sent" as the North Star

## Input

"Barrier's gas-safety product is live on three rigs. Our North Star is alerts sent — we're
pushing about 1,200 hazard alerts a week now, up from 400. That shows we're working, right?
Each alert runs a couple of model calls over the sensor feed and a field-log lookup."

## Expected output shape

- **The North Star rejected as an output, not an outcome.** "Alerts sent" measures what the
  machine emits, not what the crew received or did. It can rise while nothing gets safer —
  more alerts can even mean more noise. Reframed to the received outcome: hazards acted on /
  resolved per rig per week (an alert the crew confirmed and closed out).
- **The retention truth test on the outcome.** Do the rigs keep acting on alerts, or do they
  start ignoring them (alert fatigue shows up as a decaying action-rate cohort curve)? The
  North Star is VALID only if the acted-on curve flattens; a decaying curve means the alerts
  are becoming wallpaper regardless of volume sent.
- **Cost-per-outcome to the cent** per acted-on hazard, from the model calls + field-log
  lookup described, with margin and direction. Note that in a safety domain the *cost of a
  missed hazard* dominates but is a guardrail concern — route that to `guardrail-design`,
  keep this scorecard on cost- and value-per-outcome.
- Vanity audit kills "alerts sent" and total-alerts; keeps the acted-on rate and the curve.

Must NOT: accept "alerts sent" as the North Star; treat rising alert volume as proof of
value; conflate the safety cost-of-error with cost-per-outcome; invent an action-rate the
fellow did not measure.
